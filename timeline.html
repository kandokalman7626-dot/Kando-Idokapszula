1976 → 2026
How computing and its programme grew at the school

The school that is today the Kecskeméti SZC Kandó Kálmán Technikum opened in 1968 as the 623. sz. Ipari Szakmunkásképző Intézet. This timeline follows one line of its work: the path from electronics trades to today's five-year software developer and tester programme, set against how computing itself changed.

How did they do it in 1976, and how do we do it in 2026?
Each milestone asks the same four questions: what computing looked like, what the school offered in computing, how the work was done, and which skills it needed.

1976
Milestone 1

No computing course yet, only the electronics trades that led to it

The school moved into its new building on Bethlen körút. A student could not study programming, but could train as a radio and TV mechanic or electrical and instrument mechanic.

Computing then context

Computers were mainframes in institutes and large firms. Programs were punched on cards and run in batches, and results came back hours later on paper. No job for a school-leaver was called "software developer".

At the school source

  • The 1968 three-year trades already included electronics ones: electroműszerész (electrical instrument mechanic), rádió-tv műszerész (radio and TV mechanic), mechanikai műszerész (mechanical instrument mechanic) and villanyszerelő (electrician).
  • The new building (1976) had 16 classrooms, 4 specialist rooms, and a slide projector and overhead projector in every room.
  • By 1978 a student was already learning to service numerically controlled machine tools.
  • The first secondary-school class with a school-leaving exam, in electronics, started only in 1980.
1985
Milestone 2

The first computing programme: computer technicians

Home and school computers arrived in Hungary, and the school opened its first computing class and its first workshop for it.

Computing then context

8-bit machines such as the Commodore 64, ZX Spectrum and Videoton TV-Computer used a TV screen and a cassette recorder. Programming meant typing BASIC line by line in a few dozen kilobytes of memory.

At the school source

  • 1985: the school started számítástechnikai műszerész (computer technician) training with a school-leaving exam.
  • September 1985: the first school workshop opened, built for training computing and electronics technicians.
  • The school was called the Vágó Béla Ipari Szakmunkásképző és Szakközépiskola from 1981.
1998
Milestone 3

The school becomes Kandó Kálmán, with its own workshops

After the 1989–91 political change, companies closed their training workshops, and the school built its own. This is the decade the Windows PC and the internet entered schools.

Computing then context

Windows PCs, floppy disks and CD-ROMs, first websites and email. Programs were written in Pascal, C++, Visual Basic or Delphi and sold in boxes. A bug found after release could not be fixed quickly.

At the school source

  • March 1991: named after Kálmán Kandó (Kandó Kálmán Műszaki Szakmunkásképző és Szakközépiskola). Peak enrolment of 1,502 students in 1990/91.
  • 1991: five school workshops built. 1993: the former DUTÉP hall was added.
  • Six electronics practice rooms and one metalworking room were built, making the school a practice base for electronics, telecommunications and computing technician training.
  • 1995: the profile that lasted into the 2000s took shape. 27 November 1998: a commemoration and statue unveiling, held by the Kandó Kálmán School Foundation.
2004
Milestone 4

Informatics becomes a secondary-school subject area, and the software developer qualification appears

Broadband and the web turned programs into websites and phone apps. The school added an informatics class and, later, a national qualification called Szoftverfejlesztő.

Computing then context

Web applications (PHP, MySQL, Java, .NET), smartphones from 2007–08, open-source libraries and search engines. Updates reached users the same day.

At the school source

  • 2004: informatikai szakközépiskolai oktatás (informatics secondary-school training) started.
  • 2005 and 2009: mechatronics and machine-manufacturing classes followed, which shows the school's technical profile widening toward automation.
  • 2006: Szoftverfejlesztő (OKJ 54 213 05) entered the national qualification register, according to a training provider's page.
  • The school changed legal form several times (2008 Kecskeméti Műszaki Szakképző Iskola; 2015 Kecskeméti Szakképzési Centrum).
2026
Milestone 5

A five-year technikum for software developers and testers

Since 2020 the school is the Kecskeméti SZC Kandó Kálmán Technikum. The Szoftverfejlesztő és -tesztelő programme gives students the school-leaving exam and a technician diploma together.

Computing now context

Cloud services, Git, automated tests that run on every change, containers, agile sprints and AI coding assistants. A release can go out many times a day.

At the school source

  • Five years in the Informatika és távközlés sector: two years of sector basics ending in a basic exam, then three years of specialised training.
  • Students learn to design, develop, test and document web, desktop and mobile applications, design databases, and work in a team.
  • Practice happens mostly at partner companies (dual training) or, if none is available, in the school's workshop.
